01 / Story

How I got here. Design for people with a sensory deficit, and it opens up for everyone.

Feeling music

2005 · NUS PhD, Singapore

My PhD asked how deaf people could enjoy music. A haptic chair that carries sound through the body, plus a visual display of the music, became the start of everything that followed.

Enhancing musical experience HCI journal ↗HapticChair AHL ↗

A lab of my own

2011 · SUTD, Singapore

As founding faculty at SUTD I started the Augmented Human Lab. Its idea, later the book Assistive Augmentation: build for a sensory deficit and the result often helps everyone.

Assistive Augmentation Springer 2018 ↗All projects AHL ↗

Objects that listen

2013 · SUTD, Singapore

A small sticker that makes everyday objects respond to sound: hear an event, then light up or notify. Useful for anyone, essential for people who cannot hear it.

StickEar UIST 2013 ↗Project AHL ↗

Reading with a finger

2015 · MIT Media Lab + SUTD

With Pattie Maes' group at MIT we built FingerReader: a ring camera that reads printed text aloud as a blind user traces it, with vibration to keep them on the line.

FingerReader CHI 2015 ↗FingerReader2.0 IMWUT ↗

The body as interface

2015 → 2019 · SUTD + Auckland

We kept finding new places to interact: inside the mouth, on body hair, under the feet, and just above a smartwatch. Each opened discreet input or feedback.

ChewIt CHI 2019 ↗M-Hair UIST 2019 ↗

Curious classrooms

2018 · ABI, Auckland

At the Auckland Bioengineering Institute we put real sensors into science and coding lessons. Kiwrious grew into a company and won the Velocity $100K social entrepreneurship prize.

Interactive worksheets IDC 2021 · Best short paper ↗Scratch and Sense SIGCSE 2022 ↗

AiSee

2020 · Auckland → Singapore

An ear-worn camera that blind shoppers ask about what they hold. AiSee won a Meta Llama Impact Innovation Award and a Prototypes for Humanity award in 2024, and is now a startup.

AiSee IMWUT 2020 ↗Project AHL ↗

Measuring VR sickness

2022 · NUS, Singapore

We built a tool to record what players see and feel in commercial VR games, then released VR.net, a real-world dataset for motion sickness research that won best paper at IEEE VR 2024.

VRhook UIST 2022 ↗VR.net IEEE VR 2024 ↗

Hearing the view

2024 · NUS, Singapore

Descriptions are not the same as experiencing a view. We use AI to turn far-off scenery into sound, so blind and low-vision people can sense a landscape.

SonicVista IMWUT 2024 ↗Scene2Audio CHI 2026 ↗

Gut feelings

2025 · NUS, Singapore

Can a wearable read and shape gut feelings, literally? We sense abdominal sounds and play audio-haptic feedback back to the stomach. VisceroHaptics received a CHI 2026 Honorable Mention.

GutIO CHI EA 2025 ↗VisceroHaptics CHI 2026 ↗

A wearable fact-checker

2026 · NUS + MIT

Back with Pattie Maes' group: a real-time wearable fact-checker whose small nudges reduced how much people believed false information they heard.

Feeling the Facts CHI 2026 ↗Factually AHL ↗

Augmentation at scale

Now · NUS + AiSee, Singapore

Today I direct the Centre for Holistic Inquiry into Lifelong Learning, serve as Vice-Dean of Communications at NUS Computing, and keep building AiSee with the lab.
